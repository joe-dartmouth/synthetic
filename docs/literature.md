## How this registry works

Each entry is one bullet in this order: the key, the canonical identifier, the intake status, then
the authors, title and venue as the source gives them and the source sections that cite it.

```
- `key-YYYY` · [label spelling the identifier](https://canonical-url) · `intake-status` · Authors, "Title", venue. Cited in: ...
```

**Keys** are the lowercase first-author surname, a hyphen and the four-digit publication year, with
a trailing letter when two works share both. Where the source names no author, the key uses the
work's own short name (KIPPS, OntoBook) or a distinctive word from its title, method, publisher or
subject. Where the source gives no year, the year comes from the identifier when the identifier
encodes it (an arXiv ID's first four digits are the year and month, so `2507` is July 2025);
otherwise the key ends in `-nd` (no date) and the entry says the year is not given.

**Identifiers** are links whose label spells the identifier. A work for which neither source gives
an identifier reads `identifier not given in source` in place of the link. Titles in quotation marks are verbatim from
the citing source, ellipses included; the Santos et al. and `seedat-2024` titles, and those of the
works ADR `research/classifier-panel` and `docs/RESEARCH.md` section 7 cite, are verbatim from
their verified metadata.

**Intake status** records how far the document itself has been taken in:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `not-fetched` | Identifier recorded; document not retrieved |
| `fetched-not-read` | Document retrieved and stored; not read |
| `read-not-extracted` | Read; claims not yet extracted into the docs |
| `extracted` | Read, and the claims the docs rely on are checked against it |

An entry is `not-fetched` unless it says otherwise, and a claim attributed to a `not-fetched` work
is the citing source's claim, not a checked one.

## Ontologies, reasoners and knowledge graphs

- `kazakov-2014` · [doi:10.1007/s10817-013-9296-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10817-013-9296-3) · `extracted` · Kazakov, Krötzsch, Simančík, "The Incredible ELK: From Polynomial Procedures to Efficient Reasoning with EL Ontologies", Journal of Automated Reasoning 53(1):1-61 (2014; online 2013-11-17). ELK classifies ontologies in the EL family of description logics, on which the OWL EL profile is based: its consequence-based calculus derives every entailed subsumption between atomic concepts, top and bottom and only entailed ones (sound and complete, section 3), in polynomial time (section 3.5), and transitive reduction turns the result into a taxonomy of direct subsumptions (section 5.4). The release it describes, ELK 0.3.2, supports EL+bottom (EL with the bottom concept), a fragment of OWL EL, not every OWL EL feature (sections 2, 7 and 10). Read from the authors' accepted manuscript, hosted by TU Dresden's ICCL (2026-11-05); metadata checked against Crossref (2026-11-05). Cited in: `docs/RESEARCH.md` section 10.
- `jackson-2019` · [doi:10.1186/s12859-019-3002-3](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12859-019-3002-3) · `extracted` · Jackson, Balhoff, Douglass, Harris, Mungall, Overton, "ROBOT: A Tool for Automating Ontology Workflows", BMC Bioinformatics 20:407 (2019). ROBOT is a command-line tool and Java library over the OWL API for OBO ontology workflows; its `reason` command runs a chosen reasoner (large ontologies typically ELK), halts on an incoherent ontology and asserts every direct inferred subClassOf axiom; `relax` asserts subClassOf axioms entailed by equivalence axioms; `reduce` removes redundant subClassOf axioms; a typical release chains `reason`, `relax`, `reduce` and `annotate` to build the OWL release and converts it to OBO. Read from its Europe PMC full text (2026-11-05). Cited in: `docs/RESEARCH.md` section 10.
