# Filling clinical coverage gaps with medical knowledge

## BKO status update

Partiban Anathurai · Shirley Asangi · Joe Laws · Krish Sapru

ENGG 119 · 2026-11-09

**Question:** can medical knowledge, held as a Bayesian Knowledge-driven Ontology, find coverage gaps in clinical data and generate synthetic records that improve prediction for underrepresented groups?

---

<!-- _class: glossary -->

## Glossary

| Term | Meaning | Term | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **BKO** | Bayesian Knowledge-driven Ontology: ontologies fused with the data | **CTGAN** | Conditional Tabular GAN: neural table generator |

---

## How BKO works

1. **Represent:** fuse MONDO and HPO with the dataset into a BKB, weighting each statement by how often the data shows it.
2. **Detect:** find groups (age band × sex × label) whose own records leave medically expected patterns unsupported.
3. **Generate:** fill each gap group with new records, either by
   - an **LLM** (`bko`), prompted with the group, its gaps and summary rates, never an individual record; or
   - **direct draws from the BKB** (`bko-direct`): no LLM, no cost.
4. **Validate:** keep only records that pass consistency and range checks.
5. **Evaluate:** add the records to the training data and score recall and AUC-PR on held-out patients, beside every comparison method.

Every other method learns from the training rows alone. BKO also brings ontology structure, so it can fill a group that has **no rows at all**.

## E6: a removal test on real hospital data

**Data:** UCI Diabetes 130 hospitals, 69,283 adult first encounters, label = readmitted within 30 days (9.0%).

**Target group:** men aged 60-79 who were readmitted. Two conditions, 100 holdouts each, 16 methods:

- **98.5% removal:** 19 true rows stay. Does knowledge help when only a few cases were collected? **Complete.**
- **100% removal:** no true rows stay. Can knowledge stand in for cases never collected? **Complete.**

**What 100% models:** a coverage gap, not bad data. Examples:
- readmissions recorded at another hospital;
- outcomes lost to follow-up;
- small cells suppressed for privacy;
- a model moved to a new population.

## E6 results: BKO is best with no data, tied with a few rows

Removed-group recall, mean of 100 holdouts per condition; `real` is the ceiling.

| Method | 100%: LR | 100%: HGB | 98.5%: LR | 98.5%: HGB |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `real` (ceiling) | 0.556 | 0.585 | 0.556 | 0.585 |
| **`bko-direct`** | **0.538** | **0.359** | **0.489** | 0.207 |
| ADASYN | 0.066 | 0.000 | 0.483 | 0.078 |
| CTGAN | 0.478 | 0.274 | 0.477 | **0.282** |
| SMOTE | 0.066 | 0.000 | 0.426 | 0.073 |
| Gaussian copula | 0.351 | 0.045 | 0.355 | 0.051 |
| `bko` (LLM) | 0.308 | 0.045 | 0.332 | 0.049 |
| CLLM | 0.282 | 0.087 | 0.315 | 0.120 |
| CART / BN | 0.066 | 0.000 | 0.175 / 0.136 | 0.051 / 0.020 |
| none, TVAE | 0.066 | 0.000 | 0.069 | 0.001 |

- **100%:** `bko-direct` recovers **97% of the true rows' recall** and beats CTGAN (+0.059 [+0.011, +0.108]), with no AUC-PR cost.
- **98.5%:** the copiers return. `bko-direct` **ties** ADASYN (+0.006 [-0.014, +0.026]) and CTGAN, and beats SMOTE (+0.063 [+0.013, +0.112]).
- **Knowledge vs examples:** both BKO arms beat CLLM on recall at 100% removal; the LLM arm ties it at 98.5%.

## How `bko-direct` builds a record

1. **Concept rates:** for each diagnosis and lab concept, `share = (n_cell·p_cell + 20·p_group) / (n_cell + 20)`. With the group empty, this is the readmitted-patient rate.
2. **Concepts:** draw each concept present or absent independently, at its share.
3. **Fields:**
   - sex and label are set to the target group's;
   - age comes from a readmitted patient aged 60-79;
   - each concept field matches its drawn concept;
   - every other field is copied **independently** from a different random readmitted patient.
4. **Validate:** all 126,900 rows passed.

**Why it works:** LR adds up feature effects, so real values from the right patients move every feature the right way. **Its limit:** independent draws break correlations between fields, so HGB gains less (0.359 vs 0.585). **Its assumption:** the missing group resembles other patients with the same outcome.

## E7: does the E6 result hold beyond logistic regression?

Removed-group recall, mean of 100 holdouts, under six classifiers (`docs/EVIDENCE.md`, E7; same rows, no new generation).

| Method | LR | HGB | RF | ET | MLP | NB |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **100%:** `real` (ceiling) | 0.556 | 0.585 | 0.579 | 0.475 | 0.569 | 0.603 |
| **100%: `bko-direct`** | **0.538** | **0.359** | **0.404** | 0.292 | 0.319 | **0.591** |
| **100%:** CTGAN | 0.478 | 0.274 | 0.350 | 0.302 | 0.334 | 0.530 |
| **100%:** `none` | 0.066 | 0.000 | 0.020 | 0.065 | 0.037 | 0.072 |
| **98.5%: `bko-direct`** | 0.489 | 0.207 | 0.316 | 0.268 | 0.287 | 0.532 |
| **98.5%:** CTGAN | 0.477 | 0.282 | 0.353 | 0.305 | 0.344 | 0.532 |
| **98.5%:** ADASYN | 0.483 | 0.078 | 0.091 | 0.108 | 0.193 | 0.541 |

- **Every model** reads `bko-direct` above `none` at both levels, each interval above 0 (+0.20 to +0.52).
- **100% vs CTGAN:** `bko-direct` higher under LR, HGB, RF and NB; tied under ET and MLP.
- **98.5% vs CTGAN:** lower under HGB and ET; tied under the other four.
- **ADASYN's tie is a regression effect:** under the tree models and the MLP its recall falls to 0.08-0.19.
- **`bko` (LLM) vs CLLM:** lower under HGB, RF, ET and MLP; higher under NB.
- **Calibration:** `none` underpredicts the removed group's risk under every model; `real` sits within 0.0075 of 0.

## Privacy (E8): distance and membership inference

**What leaves the machine:** CLLM sends 20 real patient rows per API call; `bko` sends aggregates and ontology statements only; `bko-direct`, CTGAN and the classical methods run fully locally.

**98.5% removal, 100 holdouts.** DCR: higher is more private (real test patients sit at 0.0031). MIA AUC on the removed group's 19 kept patients: 0.5 is chance.

| | **`bko-direct`** | ADASYN | SMOTE | CART | CTGAN | `bko` | CLLM | `real` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DCR, 5th pct | **0.0194** | **0.0000** | 0.0098 | 0.0143 | 0.0166 | 0.0160 | 0.0164 | 0.0123 |
| MIA AUC, removed group | 0.621 | **1.000** | 0.668 | 0.744 | 0.509 | 0.516 | 0.613 | 0.510 |
| Leaks more than `real`? | **yes** | **yes** | yes | yes | no | no | no (holds 0) | n/a |

- **Outside the removed group, no method leaks:** every arm's attack AUC sits within 0.0024 of `real`'s, at both levels.
- **ADASYN** exposes all 19 kept patients (AUC 1.000, TPR 1.000 at 1% false positives).
- **`bko-direct` leaks some too:** it copies field values from those 19, so the attacker scores 0.621 (+0.111 [+0.022, +0.201]). Its TPR at 1% does not differ from `real`'s.
- **100% removal:** no group members remain to attack. Every BKO arm's mean distance sits farther than `real`'s on every quantile; `bko-direct` farther than CTGAN (+0.0030 [+0.0026, +0.0035]).
- **Caveat:** no method here offers formal differential privacy.

## E9 and G7: what the ontology logic and the BKB's likelihood add

**E9, ELK.** Classifying MONDO and HPO with the ELK reasoner adds 57 ancestor pairs to HPO, none to MONDO. **None joins two mapped concepts**, so the hierarchy BKO uses is unchanged.

**E9, `bko-logic`:** `bko-direct` with the data's concept rates removed; it draws concepts from the ontology's constraints alone.

| Removed-group recall (LR) | 100% | 98.5% |
|---|---|---|
| `bko-direct` | **0.538** | **0.489** |
| `bko-logic` | 0.239 | 0.233 |
| `none` | 0.066 | 0.069 |

- Logic alone lifts recall +0.173 [+0.152, +0.194] over `none` at 100%, and its rows sit 5x farther from patients (DCR 0.093).
- The data's concept rates add the rest: `bko-logic` trails `bko-direct` by 0.299 [0.272, 0.326].

**G7 on Diabetes.** The BKB's likelier half of `bko`'s rows is more faithful (quality +0.066 [+0.059, +0.073]), so the curated arm `bko-curated` ran: recall 0.362 against `bko`'s 0.332 (+0.030 [+0.008, +0.052]). It still trails `bko-direct`, ADASYN and CTGAN. Cost $80.56.

## E10: perturbing `bko-direct`'s identifying fields

**Goal:** move `bko-direct`'s rows farther from real patients without losing recall, by changing its 19 unmapped fields (race, counts, drugs) and never its concepts, age, sex or label.

**LLM rewrite (pilot, $2.75):** rows stayed coherent and on quota, but the rewrite failed its pre-set gate: on 1 of 4 holdouts its rows sat *closer* to patients than `bko-direct`'s. The prompt returns to design.

**PRAM (full runs, 100 holdouts each, free):** redraws about 4 of the 19 fields per row, locally.

| PRAM minus `bko-direct` | 98.5% removal | 100% removal |
|---|---|---|
| Removed-group recall, LR | +0.028 [+0.006, +0.050] | +0.000 [-0.012, +0.012] |
| Removed-group recall, HGB | **+0.109 [+0.071, +0.147]** | +0.001 [-0.027, +0.029] |
| Utility contrasts above 0 | 14 of 24 | 0 of 24 (all hold 0) |
| DCR 5th pct, MIA | no detectable change | no detectable change |

- **PRAM costs nothing and helps at 98.5%**, most under HGB (0.207 to about 0.316).
- **It is not a privacy gain:** distances and attack scores did not move.

## Why BKO, even at equal accuracy

- **Works with no data:** it fills a group that has no rows, where SMOTE, ADASYN, TVAE and CART add nothing and the BN 1-4 rows. Holds under 4 of 6 classifiers against CTGAN, ties the other 2 (E7).
- **Private at equal accuracy:** with 19 rows kept, it ties ADASYN under LR while ADASYN exposes all 19 patients to a membership attack (attack AUC 1.000, against 0.621 for `bko-direct`).
- **No record-level disclosure:** the LLM path sends aggregates only, and `bko-direct` runs on-device.
- **Finds the gaps:** E2 recovered the removed group's gaps, giving a data team an audit it can act on.
- **Explainable and replayable:** each row traces to a gap, its concepts and a logged draw or prompt; the `trace` command and the app now show it per record.
- **Cheap:** $0 for `bko-direct`, $0.000136 per record with Haiku. No generator training.

**Open caveats**
- The win comes from the BKB's data rates, not the LLM or the logic alone: `bko` trails `bko-direct` by 0.23, and `bko-logic` by 0.30.
- At 98.5% removal, `bko-direct` leaks more than `real` to a membership attack on the 19 kept patients (less than ADASYN, CART or SMOTE).
- CTGAN leads under HGB and ET at 98.5% removal; PRAM narrows the HGB gap.
- UCI was neutral: the value is specific to coverage gaps.
