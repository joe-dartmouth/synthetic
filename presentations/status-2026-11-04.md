# Filling clinical coverage gaps with medical knowledge

## BKO status update

Partiban Anathurai · Shirley Asangi · Joe Laws · Krish Sapru

ENGG 119 · 2026-11-04

**Question:** can medical knowledge, held as a Bayesian Knowledge-driven Ontology, find coverage gaps in clinical data and generate synthetic records that improve prediction for underrepresented groups?

---

<!-- _class: glossary -->

## Glossary

| Term | Meaning | Term | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **BKO** | Bayesian Knowledge-driven Ontology: ontologies fused with the data | **CTGAN** | Conditional Tabular GAN: neural table generator |
| **BKB** | Bayesian Knowledge Base: the BKO's probabilistic store | **TVAE** | Tabular Variational Autoencoder: neural table generator |
| **MONDO** | Mondo Disease Ontology (diseases) | **GAN** | Generative Adversarial Network |
| **HPO** | Human Phenotype Ontology (findings) | **SMOTE** | Synthetic Minority Oversampling: interpolates real rows |
| **ICD-9** | Intl. Classification of Diseases, 9th rev. | **ADASYN** | Adaptive Synthetic sampling (SMOTE variant) |
| **LLM** | Large language model (Haiku 5.5) | **CART** | Classification and Regression Trees |
| **CLLM** | Curated LLM: LLM shown real rows writes more | **Copula** | Gaussian copula: columns + correlations |
| **LR** | Logistic regression (criteria model) | **BN** | Bayesian network: learned column graph |
| **HGB** | Histogram gradient boosting (trees) | **DCR** | Distance to closest record (privacy) |
| **Recall** | Share of true positives caught | **AUC-PR** | Area under precision-recall curve |
| **Holdout** | One train/test split; we average 100 | **E1-E6, G6, G7** | Our experiment IDs |

---

## How BKO works

1. **Represent:** fuse MONDO and HPO with the dataset into a BKB, weighting each statement by how often the data shows it.
2. **Detect:** find groups (age band × sex × label) whose own records leave medically expected patterns unsupported.
3. **Generate:** fill each gap group with new records, either by
   - an **LLM** (`bko`), prompted with the group, its gaps and summary rates, never an individual record; or
   - **direct draws from the BKB** (`bko-direct`): no LLM, no cost.
4. **Validate:** keep only records that pass consistency and range checks.
5. **Evaluate:** add the records to the training data and score recall and AUC-PR on held-out patients, beside every comparison method.

Every other method learns from the training rows alone. BKO also brings ontology structure, so it can fill a group that has **no rows at all**.

## E6: a removal test on real hospital data

**Data:** UCI Diabetes 130 hospitals, 69,283 adult first encounters, label = readmitted within 30 days (9.0%).

**Target group:** men aged 60-79 who were readmitted. Two conditions, 100 holdouts each, 16 methods:

- **98.5% removal:** 19 true rows stay. Does knowledge help when only a few cases were collected? **Complete.**
- **100% removal:** no true rows stay. Can knowledge stand in for cases never collected? **Complete.**

**What 100% models:** a coverage gap, not bad data. Examples:
- readmissions recorded at another hospital;
- outcomes lost to follow-up;
- small cells suppressed for privacy;
- a model moved to a new population.

## E6 results: BKO is best with no data, tied with a few rows

Removed-group recall, mean of 100 holdouts per condition; `real` is the ceiling.

| Method | 100%: LR | 100%: HGB | 98.5%: LR | 98.5%: HGB |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `real` (ceiling) | 0.556 | 0.585 | 0.556 | 0.585 |
| **`bko-direct`** | **0.538** | **0.359** | **0.489** | 0.207 |
| ADASYN | 0.066 | 0.000 | 0.483 | 0.078 |
| CTGAN | 0.478 | 0.274 | 0.477 | **0.282** |
| SMOTE | 0.066 | 0.000 | 0.426 | 0.073 |
| Gaussian copula | 0.351 | 0.045 | 0.355 | 0.051 |
| `bko` (LLM) | 0.308 | 0.045 | 0.332 | 0.049 |
| CLLM | 0.282 | 0.087 | 0.315 | 0.120 |
| CART / BN | 0.066 | 0.000 | 0.175 / 0.136 | 0.051 / 0.020 |
| none, TVAE | 0.066 | 0.000 | 0.069 | 0.001 |

- **100%:** `bko-direct` recovers **97% of the true rows' recall** and beats CTGAN (+0.059 [+0.011, +0.108]), with no AUC-PR cost.
- **98.5%:** the copiers return. `bko-direct` **ties** ADASYN (+0.006 [-0.014, +0.026]) and CTGAN, and beats SMOTE (+0.063 [+0.013, +0.112]).
- **Knowledge vs examples:** both BKO arms beat CLLM on recall at 100% removal; the LLM arm ties it at 98.5%.

## How `bko-direct` builds a record

1. **Concept rates:** for each diagnosis and lab concept, `share = (n_cell·p_cell + 20·p_group) / (n_cell + 20)`. With the group empty, this is the readmitted-patient rate.
2. **Concepts:** draw each concept present or absent independently, at its share.
3. **Fields:**
   - sex and label are set to the target group's;
   - age comes from a readmitted patient aged 60-79;
   - each concept field matches its drawn concept;
   - every other field is copied **independently** from a different random readmitted patient.
4. **Validate:** all 126,900 rows passed.

**Why it works:** LR adds up feature effects, so real values from the right patients move every feature the right way. **Its limit:** independent draws break correlations between fields, so HGB gains less (0.359 vs 0.585). **Its assumption:** the missing group resembles other patients with the same outcome.

## Privacy

**What leaves the machine**

- **CLLM** sends 20 real patient rows per API call.
- **`bko`** sends aggregates and ontology statements only.
- **`bko-direct`**, CTGAN and the classical methods run fully locally.

**DCR, 5th percentile** (mean of 100 holdouts; higher is more private; real test patients sit at 0.0031)

| Condition | **`bko-direct`** | ADASYN | SMOTE | CTGAN | Copula | `bko` | CLLM |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100% removal | **0.0194** | no rows | no rows | 0.0164 | 0.0229 | 0.0211 | 0.0171 |
| 98.5% removal | **0.0194** | **0.0000** | 0.0098 | 0.0166 | 0.0225 | 0.0160 | 0.0164 |

- **At equal recall, `bko-direct` is the private choice:** ADASYN ties it at 98.5% removal, but ADASYN's closest rows sit **on top of** real patients. They are the 19 kept patients, interpolated.
- `bko-direct` keeps the same distance in both conditions. It is farther than CTGAN at 100% removal (+0.0030 [+0.0026, +0.0035]).
- Exact test copies: at most 1 added row per arm (`bko`'s replica copied 16 at 98.5% removal).
- **Caveat:** each value `bko-direct` writes is a real patient's value, though each field comes from a different patient. No method here offers formal differential privacy.

## Why BKO, even at equal accuracy

- **Works with no data:** it fills a group that has no rows, where SMOTE, ADASYN, TVAE and CART add nothing and the BN 1-4 rows.
- **Private at equal accuracy:** with 19 rows kept, it ties ADASYN on recall while ADASYN's rows copy real patients.
- **No record-level disclosure:** the LLM path sends aggregates only, and `bko-direct` runs on-device.
- **Finds the gaps:** E2 recovered the removed group's gaps, giving a data team an audit it can act on.
- **Explainable and replayable:** each row traces to a gap, its concepts and a logged draw or prompt.
- **Cheap:** $0 for `bko-direct`, $0.000136 per record with Haiku. No generator training.

**Open caveats**
- The win comes from the BKB's structure, not the LLM: at 100% removal `bko` trails `bko-direct` by 0.23 and CTGAN by 0.17.
- CTGAN leads under HGB at 98.5% removal (0.282 against 0.207).
- Most row-adding methods lower AUC-PR on the group; `bko-direct` does not at 100% removal (-0.013 at 98.5%).
- CLLM keeps a slightly higher AUC-PR than the LLM `bko` arm at both removal levels.
- UCI was neutral: the value is specific to coverage gaps.
