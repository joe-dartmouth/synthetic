# `bko.gaps`: gap detection

Find statements the ontology implies but the data does not support. Santos et al. state, as their Proposition 5.1.1, that these ungrounded statements contribute nothing to the model's distribution, so the plan uses them as the formal definition of a dataset gap. Count-based gaps are detected and evaluated separately. `bko.gaps.observe` turns a dataset into fragments, whole and per cell (ADR [`research/dataset-fragment-encoding`](../../../docs/adr/research/dataset-fragment-encoding.md)), `bko.gaps.detect` grounds them and lists count-based gaps, `bko.gaps.definitions` reads a dataset's (cell, statement) gaps under each definition of ADR [`research/bko-formal-gap-definition`](../../../docs/adr/research/bko-formal-gap-definition.md), `bko.gaps.experiment` holds E2's ablations and their reading, and `bko.gaps.scope` loads the pinned releases and their fragments for the commands that read them; `bko.gaps.definitions.completeness_request` builds the completeness request, and `bko.gaps.report` holds the report columns the commands share; [`docs/DEVELOPMENT.md`](../../../docs/DEVELOPMENT.md#gap-detection) holds the command. A caller reads a dataset's gaps under one definition with `record_cells` (a training frame) or `population_cells` (a Synthea population), `bko.gaps.detect.ontology_fragments` over the concepts its rows map, then `gaps`.

- **Dependencies:** The `data` extra (`pyproject.toml`), and `bko.ontology` with its `ontology` extra, and `bko.bkb`.
